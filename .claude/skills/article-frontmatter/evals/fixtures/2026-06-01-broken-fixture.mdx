Most discussions of AI memory focus on what the model can remember inside a single
conversation. The harder question is what happens between conversations — and who pays
the cost when memory becomes a competitive feature instead of a courtesy.

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have all shipped some form of persistent memory in 2026.
Each implementation makes a different trade-off between privacy, portability, and price.
For organizations that have built operational habits around stateless chat, the shift
to memory-by-default is not a feature update — it's a governance event.

This piece argues that the meaningful question is not "does my AI remember me" but
"who owns the record, who can subpoena it, and what happens when the vendor decides
memory is no longer free." Three vignettes from the field illustrate why nonprofits
and regulated industries should be making memory policy decisions today, not after
the first incident.
